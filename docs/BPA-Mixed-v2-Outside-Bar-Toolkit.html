Understanding the Buy Level & Sell Level

These two trade management lines mark your stop-loss placement when an outside bar triggers a setup. Here's exactly what they mean and how to read them.

Methodology ICT / Price Action
01 — Overview

What Are Outside Bars?

An outside bar occurs when the current bar's range engulfs the previous bar's range completely — the high exceeds the prior high and the low breaks below the prior low. It signals range expansion and often represents a liquidity sweep: trapped traders on both sides.

The Buy Level and Sell Level are not entry signals. They mark where your protective stop order sits after an outside bar triggers a trade. The naming describes the type of stop order, not the trade direction.

↑

Bullish Outside Bar

Swept below the prior low, closed above the prior high. Trapped sellers below. Direction bias: long.

↓

Bearish Outside Bar

Swept above the prior high, closed below the prior low. Trapped buyers above. Direction bias: short.

Why "Buy Level" for a Short?

The label describes the type of stop order, not the trade direction. You're short, so your protective stop is a buy-stop order placed above the OB high. If price hits this level, the bearish setup is negated.

04 — How It Works

The Decision Flow

Here's the step-by-step logic the script follows when evaluating an outside bar:

Step 1

Detect the Outside Bar

Current bar's high ≥ prior high AND current bar's low ≤ prior low. The bar is classified as bullish (green close) or bearish (red close).

Step 2

Check the Direction

Bearish OB + red close → short bias. Bullish OB + green close → long bias. The OB must close in the direction of the trade (not just any outside bar).

Step 3

Calculate the Bar Height

barHeight = high - low This becomes the measured-move distance for both the stop and the risk target.

Step 4

Plot the Levels

For a bearish OB: stop at high + 1 tick, risk target at high + barHeight. For a bullish OB: stop at low - 1 tick, risk target at low - barHeight.

Step 5

Follow-Through Check

On subsequent bars, the script checks if the next candle confirms the OB direction (closes above/below the OB's extreme). If confirmed, a follow-through arrow appears.

06 — Context

Why This Matters in BPA

Outside bars represent range expansion and potential traps. The OB toolkit translates raw candle data into actionable trade management:

1

Identify the Trap

Bearish OB swept highs and closed red — buyers above are trapped. Bullish OB swept lows and closed green — sellers below are trapped.

2

Define Your Risk

The stop level is one tick beyond the OB extreme. If price returns there, the trap thesis is invalidated. Clean, mechanical.

3

See the Failure Cost

The blue measured-move line shows exactly what happens if the OB was itself a trap — full range projection against your position.

4

Confirm with Follow-Through

The script marks when the next bar confirms the OB direction, giving you confidence that the trap thesis is playing out.

Remember

The naming convention describes the type of stop order, not the trade direction. "Buy Level" = where your protective buy-stop sits (you're short). "Sell Level" = where your protective sell-stop sits (you're long). The blue risk target line shows the full measured-move distance if the trade fails.
